# Why Forever Warrior rage is a coherent design

*Notes from the WoW Forever beta, September 2026. Not affiliated with Blizzard. Rage numbers and talent ranks can still move before November 4.*

The rage change is the real Warrior patch. Talents are garnish around it.

## What actually changed

Vanilla rage was a damage-to-resource converter: bigger white hits and crits printed more rage, which bought more Heroic Strikes and Executes, which printed more damage. Gear was a second rage talent.

Forever copies the later-WoW idea:

- Auto-attack rage is a **fixed amount tied to weapon speed**, not to the damage number on the swing.
- **Crits do not pay extra rage.** They still hit harder. They just do not also refill the bar.
- Heroic Strike no longer secretly props up off-hand hit. Dual Wield Specialization is supposed to replace that bug.

They did not leave the bar empty on purpose without a kit around it:

| Lever | What it does |
|---|---|
| Unbridled Wrath | 12% per rank (was 8%), **2 rage on a 2H proc** |
| Dual Wield Spec | Off-hand rage +20% and off-hand hit |
| Boundless Rage | Cap 110 / 120 / 130, not more income |
| Anger Management | Tooltip finally admits 1 rage / 3s in combat |
| Improved Cleave / Raging Blows | Spend less, not hit harder |
| Prot: block / dodge / parry rage, Focused Rage, better Bloodrage | Tank rage from *not dying* |
| Enrage | Any damage taken can proc it, not only incoming crits |
| Tactical Mastery | Baseline at 14 so stance dancing is not a 5-point tax |

Beta at 20 makes this feel worse than live 60 will. You have Charge, Bloodrage, Heroic Strike, and a lot of waiting. No Bloodthirst, no Whirlwind, no Flurry loop. The forum report of “auto → wait → press → wait” is a real level-20 experience, not proof the 60 kit is empty.

## Why this is a coherent design

### 1. It kills the Warrior compound-interest bug

Vanilla Warrior was not just “good.” Damage raised rage, rage raised ability count, ability count raised damage. Once you had world buffs and a slow 2H, nobody else was in the same scaling function. Forever is a permanent level-60 game with 10- and 20-man raids. If they keep that flywheel, Warrior owns the flavor forever and every other melee is a costume. Normalization is the only lever that actually breaks the loop instead of shaving 3% off Mortal Strike.

### 2. Crit was doing two jobs

A crit already deals more damage. Paying rage on top meant crit rating was rage *and* throughput. That is why Fury stacked crit until the bar was a firehose and then dumped Heroic Strike. Making crit “only” a damage event is how you make Strength, hit, and weapon speed mean something again. Unbridled Wrath is the replacement rage talent: it pays **on hit**, so it is a swing-timer talent, not a crit talent. That is a cleaner split.

### 3. Horizontal content needs a Warrior who exists at week one

Classic Warrior felt terrible in greens and godlike in raid loot. Forever’s whole pitch is “the world stays 1–60 and keeps getting content.” If rage scales with itemization, every new dungeon blue is a hidden class buff and early-phase Warriors are a joke. Flat rage says: a 19 in Hall of Thanes and a 60 in Hyjal press the same buttons on a similar cadence. Gear still raises damage per press. It should not also raise how often you get to press.

### 4. They moved rage onto spec identity instead of item level

Fury buys Unbridled Wrath, Dual Wield rage, and a bigger pool. Arms keeps Anger Management and stance rage. Protection gets rage from the things a tank actually does — block, dodge, parry — which Vanilla never paid. That is how you make three Warrior specs instead of “Fury because the bar fills” and “Prot because someone has to hold aggro.” Scarcity is what makes those talents worth points.

### 5. Two-hand Unbridled Wrath is the Arms apology, and it is a good one

Normalization plus “no crit rage” would have forced dual wield as the only rage engine (more swings = more procs). Paying **2 rage per 2H proc** is them saying slow weapons still belong in the resource model. That protects Arms and 2H Fury as playstyles instead of accidentally deleting them.

### 6. Stance dancing only matters when rage is scarce

Baseline Tactical Mastery plus Improved Tactical Mastery is wasted design if you are overflowing at 100 anyway. You only feel the cost of Battle → Defensive → Berserker when the 10–25 rage you would have dumped on the floor actually matters. Scarce rage is what makes the stance game a game.

### 7. Boundless Rage is not a generation buff, and that is the point

Raising the cap to 130 does not print rage. It lets you *store* a Charge + Bloodrage + incoming-damage spike for an Execute window instead of bleeding it off the top. Vanilla’s 100 cap punished you for playing well for five seconds. A bigger bucket with a slower tap is “plan the dump,” not “the class is overtuned.”

### 8. They are designing against the parse, not the fantasy

World buffs are already stripped from raids. Armor debuffs no longer fully stack. Bloodthirst coefficient is down. Rage normalization sits in that same pile: Forever does not want a 40-man, flask-and-Dragonslayer Warrior that turns the meter off for everyone else. The class fantasy they are keeping is “I hit things and spend the heat.” The fantasy they are cutting is “my item level is a resource multiplier nobody else has.”

## The honest limit

Normalization is coherent. **Zero rage on crit** is the part that still has to earn its keep at 60. Crit as pure damage is clean on paper; in the hand it can feel like the class stopped celebrating the thing it is built around. If live Fury with Unbridled Wrath, Flurry, and Dual Wield still sits on empty between Bloodthirsts, that is not philosophy — that is the numbers being low. Beta at 20 cannot answer that.

The design makes sense if the goal is a Warrior who is still a Warrior at level 20 and still a Warrior in phase-three greens, instead of a class that is a corpse until the loot arrives. That is a Forever goal. It is not a 2019 Faerlina goal. Those two games should not share a rage formula.
